# RISKS.md — Risk Register

Review at every phase boundary. Update when a risk materializes, retires, or changes shape. Severity = Likelihood × Impact (H/M/L). Ordered by severity, then by how early it bites.

| ID | Risk | L | I | Phase exposure | Mitigation | Trigger → Contingency |

| R-01 | **OGRE 14/Vulkan doesn't perform or won't come up on Android.** Two 2026-07-31 findings pull in opposite directions. **⬇ De-risked (V-8):** OGRE 14.5.2's Vulkan RenderSystem has genuine upstream Android support — `VK_KHR_android_surface` via `ANativeWindow`, volk dynamic loading. The path is implemented, not hypothetical, so "won't come up" is now much less likely than "won't perform". **⬆ Still sharp (V-7):** the shipping desktop build renders through **Direct3D 9** with GL and GL3Plus commented out, so there is no exercised OpenGL path to migrate from — the backend is replaced wholesale, and no reference configuration exists to compare rendering against | **L–M** | H | 1–3 | Spike A first, bounded effort, ogre-next comparison inside the spike. Expect *no* working reference configuration to compare against — the desktop reference validates game logic and content, not the render path | Gate 1 fail → **pivot to Godot 4 + solver GDExtension** (pre-committed thresholds in ROADMAP.md; solver C API preserves the pivot) |
| R-02 | **Vulkan driver fragmentation** on Mali/Adreno/Xclipse/PowerVR — "works on my phone" failures across the fleet. *Sharpened by a one-device pool: breadth evidence is currently near zero.* | H | M | 3, 6, 9 | **Cloud device farm (Firebase Test Lab) moves from nice-to-have to the primary breadth mechanism** while the physical pool is one device; driver-bug log; allow/deny list; feature-scaling within Vulkan; staged Play rollout | Failures beyond a small fraction of devices → expand deny-list; only if widespread, reconsider ANGLE/GLES fallback |
| R-03 | **Physics can't hold 2 kHz within CPU budget on midrange** (or misses budget when big.LITTLE scheduling misplaces the thread) | M | M | 1, 4 | Spike B early benchmark; big-core pinning + ADPF; NEON and multi-core force loops as tuning headroom | Spike B fail → vehicle complexity caps + physics LOD become mandatory plan items (not a project-killer) |
| R-04 | **Content licensing violation** — shipping community mods without rights; the plan calls this the most likely source of legal trouble | M | H | 3, 8, 9 | Hard constraint: GPLv3 base content only; licensing cross-cutting track; final audit in Phase 9; mod browser serves only rights-cleared content | Any doubt about an asset → treat as un-shippable until proven otherwise |
| R-05 | **Solo-dev bottleneck on human-only work** — GPU driver triage, numerical stability, threading races, netcode/NAT are where AI assistance is weakest and schedule risk concentrates | H | M | all, esp. 1, 4, 7 | AGENTS.md division-of-labor rules (AI prepares experiments, human observes/decides); phase ordering front-loads the worst of it (Spikes) | A human-only task stalls → timebox it, descope the feature, or re-order within the phase; don't let AI fake progress on it |
| R-06 | **Thermal throttling erases performance targets** in 10–20 min sessions. *Impact raised to High: the entry-tier reference device (D-013) has the least thermal headroom and the least performance margin above the 30 fps floor.* | H | **H** | 4, 6 | Design for post-throttle sustained clocks from the start; ADPF-driven quality step-down; dynamic resolution; endurance runs are Phase 4/6 exit criteria, not optional checks | Sustained targets unreachable on entry tier → drop the entry tier's feature set further, or raise the supported-device floor and record the device as a deny-list case study (DECISIONS.md) |
| R-07 | **Dependency porting quagmire** — Caelum/PagedGeometry/MyGUI are OGRE-version-locked forks; Conan has no Android path | M | M | 2 | Superbuild with pinned versions; stub-first policy (Caelum and MyGUI are slated for replacement anyway — don't over-invest in their ports) | A plugin port drags → stub it and pull its replacement forward (sky → Phase 6 item; UI → Phase 5 item) |
| R-08 | **Android lifecycle/process-death instability** — Vulkan surface loss, physics-thread teardown, state restore; fiddly human-debugging work | M | M | 2, 4 | Lifecycle sanity inside Spike A; hardening + torture-test checklist in Phase 4.4 | Persistent lifecycle crashes → dedicated stabilization pass before Phase 5; it blocks playability |
| R-09 | **Touch controls don't feel good** — the hardest design problem; huge command set (driving + hydraulics + cranes + aircraft) on glass | M | M | 5 | Contextual mode-based layouts, sliders for machinery, twin-stick crane overlays, gamepad as first-class alternative; playtest-revise cycles built into exit criteria | Feel still poor after several iterations → narrow v1 scope to best-feeling vehicle classes; keep gamepad path first-class |
| R-10 | **Mobile netcode fails on real networks** — CGNAT, radio handoff, and **unreliable-packet fragmentation**: actor packets pass a ~1200-byte UDP MTU at roughly 170+ chassis nodes, and a transport that can't fragment unreliable messages forces them back onto reliable delivery (R-10's own root cause returning). *De-risked somewhat by the 2026-07-31 audit: the message model is already datagram-shaped and the client swap is one file.* | M | M | 7 | Choose a transport that fragments unreliable messages (GNS — this is now the deciding factor in D-102); relay-first topology (no P2P dependence); real-network test matrix (LTE/5G/handoff) | Sync unacceptable on LTE → reduce update rates or population caps; if on ENet, cap vehicle complexity below MTU or switch transports |
| R-11 | **Server bandwidth costs balloon** — upload grows quadratically: `n × (n−1) × 64 kbit/s` | L | M | 7, post-launch | Population caps (8–16); several small instances per VPS; bandwidth-aware server sizing | Costs exceed budget → lower slot caps; paid hosted servers as a monetization offset (D-107) |
| R-12 | **Play policy friction over downloadable scripts/mods** (AngelScript, mod downloader) | L | M | 8, 9 | Tight sandbox (no filesystem/network/native access from scripts); documented compliance argument; interpreted-bytecode-only stance | Rejection/policy change → mods become FOSS-flavor/sideload-only feature; Play build ships without mod downloader |
| R-13 | **Upstream divergence** — the fork carries its own OGRE 14 implementation while upstream lands a different one (#3418); permanent double-maintenance. *Raised to High by the hands-off decision (D-012).* | **H** | M | all | Hands-off posture accepted with eyes open; monitor-only cross-cutting track C; keep Android glue structurally separate so a rebase stays possible | #3418 merges cleanly upstream → evaluate rebasing the fork's OGRE work onto it rather than carrying both implementations forever (D-012 revisit trigger) |
| R-15 | **In-fork OGRE 1.11→14 upgrade is bigger than expected** — several major versions of API breakage, now owned entirely by the fork rather than inherited from upstream | M | H | 2 | Desktop-first sequencing (ROADMAP § 2.0) so breakage is debugged against a known-good reference before Android enters the picture; mine PR #3418 as a free map of what the upgrade touches; stub replacement-bound deps (Caelum, MyGUI) instead of porting them | Upgrade stalls → adopt #3418's merged result via rebase (D-012 revisit), or revisit Gate 1's Godot option with the new information |
| R-14 | **F-Droid/FOSS flavor breaks late** — proprietary Play Core/PAD dependency creeps into shared code | L | M | 9 | Clean asset-delivery seam from Phase 3.1; flavor split in CI once Phase 9 starts | Coupling found late → refactor behind the seam before release; F-Droid submission waits for it |

| R-16 | **Strict-aliasing UB in the solver's hot path** — `ApproxMath.h:101-132` (`fast_invSqrt`, `approx_sqrt`, `approx_pow`) punts floats through `*(float*)&i` casts. This is UB under GCC/Clang, which optimize aliasing far more aggressively on aarch64 than MSVC does on x86. Called from `ActorForcesEuler.cpp:1220,1479,1647` and `ActorManager.cpp:1664`. *Discovered in the 2026-07-31 solver audit.* | M | H | 1, 4 | Rewrite the punning as `memcpy` (C++11 — `std::bit_cast` is unavailable at `-std=c++11`) **on x86 first**, verifying no behavior change, before any ARM comparison exists to confound the result | Unexplained ARM divergence → suspect this before anything else; it is the single most likely cause of silent misbehavior |
| R-17 | **Physics results not reproducible between desktop reference and ARM** — `-ffast-math` is set in every release config (`CMakeLists.txt:89,114-116`) with no `-ffp-contract`, and aarch64 has unconditional FMA (x86-64 baseline does not). Reassociation + contraction make bit-identical parity impossible by construction, which undermines "compare ARM against the desktop reference" as a verification strategy | H | M | 1, 2.0, 4 | Establish a deterministic baseline with `-ffp-contract=off -fno-fast-math` on **both** architectures first; only then re-enable fast-math and measure the divergence as an isolated variable. Verify by trajectory tolerance, never bit-equality | Divergence exceeds tolerance even at the deterministic baseline → a real porting bug exists (check R-16 first) |
| R-18 | **A single vehicle cannot be split across cores** — the thread pool parallelizes **per actor**, never per node or per beam (`ActorManager.cpp:1250-1294`). One vehicle's beam network is solved entirely on one thread, so single-vehicle 2 kHz performance is bounded by single-core speed — the weakest dimension of an entry-tier device (D-013) | M | H | 1, 4 | Spike B measures exactly this (single-vehicle, single-core headroom) before anything depends on it; ADPF + big-core pinning maximize what one core delivers | One vehicle won't fit one entry-tier big core → intra-actor parallelism (per-node/beam decomposition) becomes new, unplanned Phase 4 work, or vehicle complexity caps tighten sharply |

| R-19 | **Terrain rendering has no working backend on Vulkan/GLES** — terrain uses a vendored, forked `OgreTerrainPSSMMaterialGenerator.cpp` (62 KB) that emits shader source from C++ strings. Its `ShaderHelperCg` and `ShaderHelperHLSL` are complete; **`ShaderHelperGLSL` and `ShaderHelperGLSLES` are stubs**. On an Android build, terrain falls through to a non-functional generator. **⬇ Downgraded from H/H on 2026-07-31:** OGRE's own release notes state that *since 13.6* `TerrainMaterialGeneratorA` internally uses the RTSS to generate shaders (work completed in 13.5, Oct 2022). Our OGRE 14 target therefore ships a Cg-free terrain generator already — the mitigation is now evidence-backed rather than hopeful. | M | **M** | 3 | **Replace** the vendored fork with modern OGRE's terrain material generator (which is RTSS-based and needs no Cg). Only implement `ShaderHelperGLSLES` if the replacement loses required features (PSSM cascades, parallax, lightmaps, composite maps) | Replacement loses needed features → implement the GLSLES helper, or write a custom shader against the existing `.otc` data model |
| R-21 | **The fork forfeits the content library that makes RoR worth playing** — ~15 years of community vehicles and terrains are unlicensed ("no license what-so-ever" per maintainers), often carry real-world logos, and frequently contain models ripped from other games. A curated fork legally cannot ship them. Only ~3 base assets (`agora`, `dafsemi`, `simple2-terrain`) are cleanly GPL-licensed. **This is the project's central bet, and it is a funding problem, not an engineering one.** | H | **H** | all, decisive by 9 | Commit early to originally-created or properly-licensed content only; treat content production as a first-class workstream with its own budget, not a Phase 9 afterthought; lean on the mod pipeline (Phase 8) so *users* can bring content the project legally cannot ship | Cannot fund enough compelling original content → **NO-GO**: shipping an empty engine is worse than not shipping. Decide this honestly before Phase 6, not at the store listing |
| R-20 | **Hydrax water rendering is a 167 KB C++ shader-string generator** — all water/underwater/depth/god-ray shaders are inline string literals in `gfx/hydrax/MaterialManager.cpp`; that is why `resources/hydrax/` holds no shader files. Vendored from a dead upstream library. Invisible to any file-based audit | M | M | 3, 6 | Treat as **replacement, not port** — like Caelum. Water can be scoped down hard for a first release (simple reflective plane) and revisited in Phase 6 | Water proves load-bearing for target content → budget a from-scratch mobile water shader rather than porting Hydrax |
